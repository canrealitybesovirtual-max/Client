# Coverage gaps found by the completeness critic (2026-11-25)

## 1. Cal/OSHA construction standards not yet in the rules (confined space, electrical/overhead lines, MEWPs/forklifts, PPE/hearing, wildfire smoke, Valley Fever, demolition, flaggers, multi-employer policy)

**Covered now:** web_calosha-programs covers IIPP, heat, reporting, Log 300, 341 permits, asbestos, lead, silica, fall protection, excavation, cranes, scaffolds, first aid, HazCom, respirators, WVPP. None of the topics listed here are covered.

**Missing:** Construction confined spaces (8 CCR 1950-1962, verify); low/high-voltage electrical safety and overhead-line clearances (8 CCR 2299+, 2700+, 2946); lockout/tagout; elevating work platforms and aerial devices (3637-3648); forklift operator training (3668); construction PPE and noise (1514-1522, 1521/5095-5100); wildfire smoke (5141.1: AQI 151/500 triggers); Valley Fever annual training in highly endemic counties (Labor Code 6709); demolition engineering survey (1734-1736); traffic control and flaggers (1598-1599, CA MUTCD); multi-employer citation policy (8 CCR 336.10), which makes the GC a controlling employer; hot work and welding (1536-1537).

**Why:** These are among the most-cited construction standards. The planned safety-program generator (MVP item 4) would produce incomplete IIPPs and Codes of Safe Practices without them, and GCs carry citation exposure for their subs' hazards under 336.10.

**Sources:** https://www.dir.ca.gov/title8/sb4.html (Title 8 Construction Safety Orders and Electrical Safety Orders); https://www.dir.ca.gov/title8/336_10.html; https://www.dir.ca.gov/title8/5141_1.html; Labor Code 6709 via https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov; Cal/OSHA Valley Fever and wildfire smoke guidance pages on dir.ca.gov/dosh

## 2. Local jobsite operating rules: construction noise and work hours, right-of-way/encroachment and street-use permits, haul routes, traffic control plans, franchised C&D haulers

**Covered now:** Only the Caltrans state-highway encroachment permit, the CALGreen 65% diversion rule and the C-31 classification scope. There is no city-level data.

**Missing:** A per-city table of permitted construction hours and noise limits, with Sunday/holiday bans and after-hours permits (e.g., LAMC 41.40, SDMC 59.5.0404, SF Police Code Art. 29; verify each); city public-works encroachment, excavation and street-use permits, including sidewalk-closure and pedestrian-access rules; haul route approvals (e.g., LADBS hillside import/export over ~1,000 cy, verify); traffic control plans under CA MUTCD; Vehicle Code 23114 covered loads; local C&D debris ordinances, deposits and exclusive franchise-hauler rules (using a non-franchise bin hauler is a citable violation in many cities).

**Why:** These ordinances cause stop-work orders, citations and neighbor complaints on almost every job, they differ by city, and the platform launches city by city (San Diego first).

**Sources:** City municipal codes (e.g., docs.sandiego.gov municode SDMC Ch. 5 Art. 9.5; codelibrary.amlegal.com LAMC; SF Police Code Art. 29); City engineering / public works right-of-way permit pages (sandiego.gov DSD, StreetsLA/BOE, SF Public Works); https://dot.ca.gov/programs/safety-programs/camutcd; LADBS haul route bulletins (ladbs.org); City franchise hauler lists (e.g., sandiego.gov/environmental-services)

## 3. Fire code during construction, fire department permits and plan review, Wildland-Urban Interface (CBC Ch. 7A) and the new Fire Hazard Severity Zone maps

**Covered now:** Title 24 Part 9 appears only in the list of code parts, plus CSLB fire-alarm and sprinkler license-scope rules. There is no fire-department process.

**Missing:** CFC Chapter 33 (2025 CFC): fire apparatus access and water supply in place before combustibles arrive, standpipes in buildings under construction once they pass ~40 ft, temporary heating, the site safety plan or fire prevention program superintendent, and fire watch (verify section numbers); hot-work and other operational permits (CFC 105.5); fire sprinkler and alarm plan review, hydrant flow tests and fire final; CRC R313 residential sprinklers; CBC 7A/CRC R337 ignition-resistant construction in FHSZs; CAL FIRE's 2025 Local Responsibility Area FHSZ map updates and local adoption; defensible space (PRC 4291) and Zone 0 status (verify); OSFM licensing for servicing fire extinguishers and fixed or water-based systems (Title 19, verify).

**Why:** The fire department must sign off before final or CO. Hot-work fires and missing water supply are common causes of loss, and the 2025 map expansion changes material and inspection requirements for many LA, Bay Area and San Diego parcels.

**Sources:** https://osfm.fire.ca.gov (FHSZ maps, Title 19, code development); California Fire Code, Title 24 Part 9 (https://www.dgs.ca.gov/BSC/Codes; ICC codes.iccsafe.org); PRC 4291 and Gov. Code 51175-51189 via leginfo; Local fire department permit and fee schedules (e.g., SDFD Fire Prevention, LAFD Fire Prevention)

**Missing:** ACA employer mandate for ALEs with 50+ FTEs (IRC 4980H; Forms 1094-C/1095-C furnish and e-file deadlines); California individual-mandate reporting to FTB by March 31 (R&TC 61000+); CalSavers registration for employers with 1+ employee and no plan (Gov. ## 5. Accessibility: CBC Chapters 11A/11B, path-of-travel calculation, CASp, federal ADA and Fair Housing

**Covered now:** None. No accessibility rule exists in any file.

**Missing:** 11B for public accommodations and commercial TIs; 11A for covered multifamily; the path-of-travel requirement on alterations, with the 20% disproportionality cap below DSA's annually adjusted valuation threshold (11B-202.4 Exc. 8; 2026 value, verify), as a calculation; CASp inspections and certificates and the litigation stay and early evaluation conference (Civ. ## 6. Hazardous waste and hazardous materials generated or stored on jobsites (DTSC, CUPA/CERS, APSA/SPCC)

**Covered now:** Only asbestos (NESHAP/Cal/OSHA/CSLB), lead RRP and the CSLB HAZ certification. Nothing on generator duties.

**Missing:** DTSC EPA ID numbers, including temporary IDs for one-time construction generation (H&SC 25160; 22 CCR 66262.12); hazardous waste determination, labeling and accumulation limits (90/180/270 days by generator size); manifests and e-Manifest with retention; treated wood waste rules (H&SC 25230+); universal waste and pre-demolition removal of PCB ballasts, fluorescent lamps and mercury devices (22 CCR 66273); contaminated soil characterization and disposal; CUPA Hazardous Materials Business Plan in CERS at 55 gal/500 lb/200 cu ft (H&SC 25507, verify for temporary sites); APSA/SPCC plans for 1,320+ gal aboveground petroleum (H&SC 25270+, 40 CFR 112); generator fees.

**Why:** Demolition and remodel waste (paint, solvents, treated wood, lamps, contaminated soil) is regulated waste. Penalties reach $70,000 per day per violation under H&SC 25189 (verify current figure), and the generator, often the contractor, stays liable.

**Sources:** https://dtsc.ca.gov (EPA ID numbers, generator requirements, treated wood waste, universal waste); https://cers.calepa.ca.gov and https://calepa.ca.gov/cupa/; https://www.epa.gov/e-manifest; 22 CCR Div. 4.5 via https://govt.westlaw.com/calregs; 40 CFR 112 via ecfr.gov

**Sources:** https://www.aqmd.gov (Rules 403, 403.1, 1113, 1166); https://www.valleyair.org (Regulation VIII, Rule 8021 Dust Control Plan); https://www.baaqmd.gov (Reg. 6 Rule 6, Reg. 8 Rule 3, CEQA Guidelines); https://www.sdapcd.org; https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/our-work/programs/portable-equipment-registration-program-perp

## 10. CEQA triggers, exemptions and mitigation monitoring (MMRP) obligations that bind the contractor

**Covered now:** None. CEQA appears only incidentally in one encroachment note.

**Missing:** Which approvals are discretionary and trigger CEQA, and which are ministerial (PRC 21080); categorical and statutory exemptions (Classes 1, 3 and 32 infill) and the 2025 infill-housing exemptions and reforms (AB 130/SB 131, verify); NOE and NOD filing and the 35/30/180-day limitation periods (PRC 21167); AB 52 tribal consultation and tribal monitors (PRC 21080.3.1); MMRP (PRC 21081.6) conditions that flow into construction documents: work hours, dust, noise, cultural and paleontological monitors, pre-construction biological surveys, and a contractor compliance log; historic-resource review before demolition of older buildings.

**Why:** Mitigation measures are enforceable permit conditions. A contractor that starts before the required surveys or monitors are in place can trigger stop-work orders and permit revocation. Owner and developer customers need trigger screening before bidding.

**Sources:** PRC 21000-21189.57 via leginfo; CEQA Guidelines, 14 CCR 15000-15387 (https://resources.ca.gov / https://lci.ca.gov); CEQAnet State Clearinghouse database (https://ceqanet.lci.ca.gov, verify host); Lead agency planning department MMRP templates

## 23. Federal and federal-aid projects (Davis-Bacon, CWHSSA, Miller Act, Buy America/BABA, DBE, SAM)

**Covered now:** Only a note that federal-aid bidders need a CSLB license at award, plus CDTFA federal-job tax treatment.

**Missing:** Davis-Bacon wage determinations and weekly certified payroll (WH-347) under the Copeland Act (40 USC 3141+, 3145; 29 CFR 3 and 5 as revised in 2023); dual compliance when California prevailing wage is higher; CWHSSA overtime and liquidated damages (40 USC 3701+); Miller Act bonds over $150,000 and payment-bond claim notice and suit deadlines (40 USC 3131-3134); Buy America/BABA (2 CFR 184; 23 CFR 635.410); DBE goals and prompt payment on Caltrans federal-aid jobs (49 CFR 26); SAM.gov registration; E-Verify (FAR 52.222-54).

**Why:** IIJA-funded local transportation and water projects bring federal rules into routine city and county contracts, and federal certified payroll is separate from DIR eCPR.

**Sources:** https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/government-contracts (Davis-Bacon, WH-347); https://sam.gov (registration and wage determinations); https://www.ecfr.gov (29 CFR 5; 2 CFR 184; 49 CFR 26); https://dot.ca.gov/programs/civil-rights/dbe

## 24. Proposition 65 warnings

**Covered now:** None.

**Missing:** Applicability to businesses with 10+ employees (H&SC 25249.6, 25249.11); occupational exposure warnings satisfied through Cal/OSHA HazCom compliance (27 CCR 25606); environmental and consumer exposure warnings at sites and for products sold or installed, such as fixtures and materials a contractor sells as a retailer (27 CCR 25600-25607.x, with 2025 short-form warning amendments and a transition to 2028, verify); 60-day notices of violation; penalties up to $2,500 per violation per day.

**Why:** Private enforcers file thousands of 60-day notices each year. Contractors that also sell fixtures or materials (they are retailers under CDTFA rules) can be targets.

**Sources:** https://oehha.ca.gov/proposition-65; https://www.p65warnings.ca.gov; 27 CCR 25600+ via https://govt.westlaw.com/calregs; https://oag.ca.gov/prop65 (60-day notice database)

## 25. Elevator, conveyance and pressure-vessel permits (DOSH Elevator Unit and Pressure Vessel Unit)

**Covered now:** Only the C-11 and C-4 license scopes and the Wage Order rule on elevators for work 60+ ft up.

**Missing:** DOSH permits to operate for elevators, escalators and wheelchair lifts, plus acceptance inspections (Labor Code 7300-7324.2; 8 CCR 3000+ Elevator Safety Orders); temporary permits for construction personnel hoists and for using permanent elevators during construction (8 CCR 1604.x, verify); the certified competent conveyance mechanic requirement (verify section); DOSH permits to operate for air tanks, boilers and fired pressure vessels, with exemptions for small portable tanks (Labor Code 7620-7771; 8 CCR 450-560, 750-797; verify thresholds); local boiler permits where they apply.

**Why:** A building cannot get its CO without the elevator permit to operate, which is often a critical-path item. Operating an unpermitted hoist or air receiver brings a Cal/OSHA citation.

**Sources:** https://www.dir.ca.gov/dosh/ (Elevator Unit; Pressure Vessel Unit); https://www.dir.ca.gov/title8/ (Elevator Safety Orders; Unfired Pressure Vessel and Boiler Safety Orders; 1604 personnel hoists); Labor Code 7300+, 7620+ via leginfo
